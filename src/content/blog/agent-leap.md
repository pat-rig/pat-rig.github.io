Sometimes, I struggle to convey with words the order of magnitude by which
Agentic Engineering expands our capabilities as compared to traditional
programming — so here is an animation.

The hills at the start are the world I worked in before agents: data science
and machine learning. Raw data is a forest you go out and fell. Preprocessing
is the mill — the same cut, applied to everything you feed it. Experiments
happen on a bench, and at the end of the day there is one hut, which is what I
could build on my own. Not a small world, and a good one to work in.

With agents, my reachable space has expanded. The places the ship passes —
CI/CD, databases, web apps, APIs and infrastructure — and the cloud platform
it lands on were all out of reach before. And I do not fly there alone: there
is always a copilot at my side, so to speak, who guides me through those
distant places and explains the technology I find there.

A ship needs fuel, and fuel needs a credit card. None of this is free — going
somewhere new still costs money and time, and the time includes learning the
concepts on the way. It is the same as in real life: we all have cars, and in
theory we could fly to most places in the world on holiday, yet we do not
drive around all day and we do not go everywhere. We prioritise. Owning the
car changed what is possible; the budget still decides where we go.

The same holds for this technology. The range is new; what remains our
responsibility is to decide where we want to go with it.
We have to evaluate that carefully before we set off, which takes knowing what is out there,
what it costs, and what it is worth to us. Having the opportunity at all is
the big thing.
